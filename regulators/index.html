Greystar and the Regulators: FTC, DOJ and State Actions

This section records actions involving Greystar by government agencies — including the Federal Trade Commission, the Department of Justice, state attorneys general and civil rights agencies — along with complaints filed with those agencies by watchdog groups. Status labels distinguish filed complaints, pending cases, consent decrees and settlements. Unless a settlement says otherwise, Greystar has not admitted wrongdoing.

2026

Greystar notifies Washington AG of data breach involving employee email account

Greystar Real Estate Partners filed a breach notice stating an unauthorized party accessed an employee email account between May 1 and May 18, 2026 and acquired files containing personal information; affected people were offered 24 months of credit monitoring. This is a mandatory disclosure, not an enforcement action; no lawsuit over this breach was found.

breach notice filed

Housing watchdog files 114 voucher-discrimination complaints against Greystar with seven state and D.C. agencies

Office of Human Rights alleging 114 violations of source-of-income laws. The complaints allege, based on undercover testing, that Greystar properties rejected Housing Choice Voucher holders, imposed unlawful conditions, and refused to count voucher assistance toward income requirements.

Arizona AG announces three settlements with Greystar over hidden rental charges and junk fees

Arizona AG Kris Mayes announced three Assurances of Discontinuance entered in Maricopa County Superior Court: one adopting the injunctive terms of the FTC/Colorado settlement (with about $1.5M in anticipated restitution to Arizona consumers from the FTC fund), and two resolving alleged Arizona Consumer Fraud Act junk-fee violations at The Julia and Avana Gilbert with a combined $100,000 in restitution.

Greystar California agrees to revise tenant screening statewide after fair housing complaint

CRD announced a settlement with Greystar California, Inc. after a complaint alleging an applicant at a Hollywood apartment complex was denied based on criminal history without an opportunity to explain. Greystar agreed to review and revise screening policies statewide, notify staff of California's criminal-history protections, train specified Southern California staff, and pay the complainant $10,000.

Greystar agrees to pay $24 million to settle FTC and Colorado hidden-fee case

Greystar agreed to pay $23 million to the FTC for consumer refunds and $1 million to Colorado, and to clearly and conspicuously display total monthly prices, including mandatory fees, in its rental listings.

settled $24M (proposed stipulated order)

Attorney General Phil Weiser, FTC announce $24M settlement with Greystar for misleading renters about costs, saddling tenants with hidden fees

Nine state attorneys general reach $7 million settlement with Greystar over algorithmic rent pricing

The attorneys general of California, Colorado, Connecticut, Illinois, Massachusetts, Minnesota, North Carolina, Oregon and Tennessee filed a proposed $7 million settlement resolving their claims against Greystar Management Services in the January 2025 RealPage litigation. Greystar agreed to stop using pricing algorithms that rely on competitors' confidential data, accept a court-appointed monitor if using third-party pricing software, and cooperate against remaining defendants.

DOJ reaches proposed settlement with Greystar to end its participation in algorithmic pricing

DOJ filed a proposed final judgment barring Greystar from using rent-pricing software trained on or using other owners' nonpublic data, from sharing competitively sensitive data with rivals, and from attending RealPage-hosted meetings, and requiring cooperation in the ongoing case. Greystar said the settlement contained no admission of wrongdoing.

Greystar to pay over $1.4M for early-termination charges on military servicemembers

DOJ alleged Greystar Management Services violated the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act by imposing early-termination charges on servicemembers who ended leases after receiving relocation orders, relying on software it knew would impose the charges automatically. Greystar agreed to set aside $1.35M for affected servicemembers and co-tenants (treble damages for those who paid), pay a $77,370 civil penalty, and adopt SCRA-compliant software and forms.

FTC and Colorado AG sue Greystar over hidden mandatory rental fees

The FTC and the State of Colorado sued Greystar Real Estate Partners and five affiliates, alleging that since at least 2019 the company advertised deceptively low base rents while failing to disclose mandatory monthly fees (pest control, valet trash, package, utility-administration and amenity fees), often until after a non-refundable application fee was paid. Claims were brought under the FTC Act, the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and the Colorado Consumer Protection Act.

resolved by stipulated order Dec 2025 (see $24M settlement)
