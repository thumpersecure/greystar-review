Leaving Costs Extra: Early-Termination Fees in the Greystar Record

From a servicemember's relocation orders to a tenant's death, charges at the end of a lease have drawn federal enforcement, a new state law and a viral video.

Leases end for many reasons: a job, a deployment, an illness, a death. What a landlord can charge when that happens has been the subject of federal enforcement, a state law and several lawsuits involving Greystar.

Servicemembers: a $1.43 million settlement

The Servicemembers Civil Relief Act lets servicemembers end a lease early after receiving relocation orders. In June 2025, the Justice Department alleged that Greystar Management Services violated the act by imposing early-termination charges on servicemembers who ended leases after orders, relying on software it knew would impose the charges automatically.

Greystar agreed to set aside $1.35 million for affected servicemembers and co-tenants, with treble damages for those who paid, to pay a $77,370 civil penalty, and to adopt compliant software and forms. The total is about $1.43 million.

A death and a new law in Colorado

When tenant Leticia Farrer died in January 2024, her family was billed about $4,140 in early-termination and concession fees at Avenida in Loveland. The lease said tenants were not released "for any reason, including death." Greystar cleared the balance after CBS Colorado reported the story. Colorado later passed "Letty's Act" (HB25-1108) to bar such fees.

Moving out early in Washington

A class action in King County Superior Court was filed on behalf of tenants who moved out early and were charged, or had deposits applied to, buy-out fees, reletting charges or future rent. Greystar removed the case to federal court, which sent it back. Plaintiffs' counsel says trial is set for February 2027. The case is pending.

Tenant accounts

Three accounts in our Letters section describe the same kind of charge. They are unverified allegations, but they show what residents say they experienced.

  • A reviewer alleges that after five months they were charged nearly three months' rent to leave early, combining a two-month termination fee and an "insufficient notice fee."
  • A Houston reviewer alleges they were not allowed to end the lease on time and were charged an extra month at double rent, and that the deposit was withheld.
  • In April 2024 the Daily Dot covered a viral TikTok, with more than 323,000 views, in which a tenant said she wanted to break a 15-month lease within 30 days over unenforced policies.

When the landlord ends the stay

Sometimes the move is not the tenant's choice. In January 2026, The Tab reported that residents of Chapter London Bridge, a student hall run by Greystar's Chapter brand, had to vacate after a major water leak caused a power outage, with restoration expected to take until at least May 2026. One student said she felt "scammed out of £20,000." Chapter offered rooms at its other sites.

In 2016, San Jose Inside reported that the City Council voted 7 to 3 to allow Greystar to demolish the 216-unit rent-controlled Reserve for a 640-unit project, displacing about 670 people. Relocation help was offered only to lower-income households, about 40 units.

What connects them

In each case the question is the same: who bears the cost when a lease ends early, and under what rules. Federal law answers it for servicemembers, and Colorado now answers it for a tenant's death. For most other situations, the answer still depends on the lease and on state law, which is why pending cases like the Washington class action matter.
