## The attorney general's RealPage suit

The central matter in the District is a government antitrust suit. On Nov. 1, 2023, the Office of the Attorney General for the District of Columbia [sued RealPage and 14 landlords]({{story:dc-ag-v-realpage-greystar-2023}}), naming Greystar Management Services, L.P. The complaint alleges the landlords collectively delegated rent-setting to RealPage's revenue-management software in violation of the District's Antitrust Act. It alleges Greystar operates about 7,730 D.C. units and used RealPage software at buildings including The Gantry and Illume.

The entry lists the case as pending as to Greystar, and no Greystar settlement was found. Greystar has settled related claims elsewhere; see the reports for [Tennessee]({{state:tennessee}}), where the private class action sits, and [California]({{state:california}}). Those are separate matters and do not resolve the District's suit.

## Voucher discrimination complaints

On July 14, 2026, Housing Rights Initiative [filed 114 complaints]({{story:hri-multistate-voucher-discrimination-complaints-2026}}) with seven state and D.C. agencies, including the D.C. Office of Human Rights. Based on undercover testing, the complaints allege that Greystar properties rejected Housing Choice Voucher holders, imposed unlawful conditions, and refused to count voucher assistance toward income requirements. They are allegations and are listed as pending. The filing also reached Hawaii; see the [Hawaii report]({{state:hawaii}}).

## Facebook housing ads and age

In July 2020, Housing Rights Initiative and an older D.C. resident sued 10 property managers, including Greystar, in [Opiotennione v. Bozzuto Management Co.]({{story:opiotennione-v-bozzuto-facebook-age-ads-2020}}) (D. Md., 8:20-cv-01956). They alleged the managers targeted Facebook apartment ads to exclude users over 50, and had earlier filed age-bias charges with D.C. and Montgomery County human rights offices in September 2019. The district case closed in July 2021 and the Fourth Circuit affirmed dismissal on standing grounds in March 2025, so the claims were not decided on their merits.

## Collections complaints on a review site

Separately, [Trustpilot reviewers]({{story:trustpilot-2023-collections-phone-harassment}}) in two separate January 2023 posts alleged that an employee in Greystar's corporate accounting and collections department repeatedly called former tenants after move-out, shouting and using what they describe as illegal tactics, and urged others to file complaints with the BBB and the D.C. Attorney General. These are unverified accounts from individual reviewers, and the entry records no agency finding.

## Reporting from Navy Yard

In August 2024, [NBC4 Washington reported]({{story:nbc4-onyx-on-first-elevator-outages-2024}}) that residents of the 14-story Onyx on First said all three elevators had been mostly out of service for days, calling it a safety and accessibility problem. Greystar, the property manager, referred questions to the owner, which said repairs were underway and elevators were running four hours a day.

## Context from national coverage

The 2026 complaint set was also covered nationally. [HousingWire]({{story:housingwire-greystar-114-voucher-complaints-2026}}) reported that testers were told vouchers were not accepted, had to cover 100% of rent, or would not count toward income minimums, and that Greystar said it is committed to fair housing. [The Guardian]({{story:guardian-greystar-civil-rights-voucher-complaints-2026}}) reported the same complaints as alleging 114 violations across six states and D.C.
